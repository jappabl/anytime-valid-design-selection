### 1.3 Is the resume a lossless replay? **CONFIRMED**, with an unasserted invariant.

`audit/sim_resume_replay.py`:

* **T1** 60 synthetic streams × 17 crash points = **1017 crash points, 0
  mismatches**: `log_pred` bit-identical (`struct.pack` hex compared),
  identical `(f, s)`, identical decision, identical stopping time, identical
  `p_hat`. Crash points deliberately include `n = 19/20/21/23/24/25` and
  `τ_stop − 1, τ_stop, τ_stop + 1`.
* **T2** stratum rotation across the seam: the original's
  `STRATA[(n-1) % 4]` for sample `n` and the resume's `STRATA[n % 4]` for
  sample `n+1` agree for all n. **No off-by-one.**
* **T4** the real log: 17 616 records, 16 reps, per-rep indices exactly
  `1..N` contiguous and in order, zero duplicates, `stratum ==
  STRATA[(n-1)%4]` for every record, and **all 16 reported
  `(decision, n, p̂)` in `results_live_prediction.txt` reproduce exactly**
  by independent replay. The reported median 1200 is arithmetically correct.

**Latent fragility (Low, no impact here).** `crossing()` replays by
`enumerate(outcomes, 1)` and never reads the record's `n` field. Permuting
a rep's records changes the reconstructed stopping time in **183/200**
synthetic trials (median shift 28 samples). Correctness rests entirely on
an unchecked invariant — that the append-only log is in per-rep sample
order. Nothing in the resume script asserts it; a two-process resume, a
partial-line write, or a manual log concatenation would silently corrupt
the reconstruction with no error.

**Deviation worth logging:** the resume re-seeds the prompt RNG
(`default_rng(9000 + 31*rep)`) rather than fast-forwarding the original
(`default_rng(42 + 3000*r)`). The resumed segment is a *fresh* prompt draw,
not a continuation. Statistically harmless (prompts are i.i.d. within
stratum) but the docstring's "deterministic replays of the log" describes
the *statistic*, not the experiment.

**Latent hazard (Low).** `resume_live_prediction.py` seeds
`spend = {"usd": 2.05}` as a hard-coded guess and breaks out of the sampling
loop on `spend > MAX_SPEND`, returning `ABSTAIN`. A budget-truncated rep is
therefore indistinguishable in the output from an `n_max` abstention. It did
not fire (16/16 UNSAFE), but it is an unlabelled failure mode in an artifact
whose headline is a count of decisions.

### 1.4 Stopping-rule consistency. **CONFIRMED.**

Live script, resume script and the offline derivation grid
(`run_overhead_law.py::run_single`) all use `StratifiedUICS(k=1,
weights=[1.0], alpha=0.05)`, push every sample into stratum 0, and check
`n >= 20 and n % 4 == 0`, testing `rejects_le` before `rejects_ge`.
Identical in all three. The closed form's `d = 1` is the right dimension
for a one-parameter Beta(1,1) mixture.

### 1.6 Are the 16 reps independent? **CONFIRMED at the outcome level.**

Every rep issues its own `client.chat.completions.create` per sample; no
outcome is shared or replayed across reps. There is **no pseudo-replication
of outcomes**. What *is* shared is the single 1 000-prompt realization
(seed 42), so p\* is one fixed number and the 16 reps are 16 draws from one
condition — not 16 independent tests of the law. The pool's own sampling
error is non-trivial: the `extreme` stratum has rate 0.736 on 250 prompts
(SE ≈ 0.028), contributing ≈ ±0.007 to p\* — comparable to the entire
width of the window in p-space (`[0.1961, 0.2079]`).

## 2. Law accounting

### 2.1 `V_rr` and `_inner_min`. **CONFIRMED — no bug (this attack fails).**

`fit_overhead_law.py::v_rr` and `run_frontier.py` call
`bench._inner_min(lam, r, w, tau)` with `lam = w = 0.25`. That is the
correct Lagrangian: `_m_of_mu` minimizes `λ_k KL(p_k‖m) + μ w_k m`
per stratum, so `_inner_min` solves `min Σ λ_k KL(p_k‖m_k)` s.t.
`Σ w_k m_k = τ`, and with `λ = w = 1/K` the objective is exactly the
round-robin per-sample growth rate `(1/K) Σ_k KL(p_k‖m_k)`. λ = 0.25 is
right, and the game solution is correctly *not* used.

`audit/sim_vrr_check.py` verifies numerically with two independent
minimizers:

| τ | `_inner_min` | SLSQP (80 restarts) | random-restart coordinate descent | printed | `V_game` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 0.019237 | 0.019237 | 0.019237 | 0.0192 | 0.049805 |
| 0.16 | 0.012678 | 0.012678 | 0.012678 | 0.0127 | 0.031106 |
| 0.17 | 0.007456 | 0.007456 | 0.007456 | 0.0075 | 0.017138 |

Agreement to 6 decimals; the minimizers `m*` agree componentwise; the
equality constraint holds to 1e-8. The printed values are correct. `d_eff`
fits do **not** shift.

### 2.5 The grid points are not independent. **BUG (Medium).**

`run_overhead_law.py` (and `run_overhead_law_code.py`) contain:

```python
for fn in [run_single, run_ui_rr, run_wsr]:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(BASE_SEED + 101)   # same seed for EVERY tau
    times = [fn(pools, tau, truth, rng) for _ in range(N_REPS)]
```

The whole τ sweep is driven by one common random stream. Demonstration
(first replication's single-stream stopping time):

| τ | shared seed 143 | independent seeds |
|---|---|---|
| 0.145 | 952 | 440 |
| 0.150 | 988 | 988 |
| 0.160 | 1524 | 1272 |
| 0.170 | 1960 | 3280 |

With the shared seed the points move smoothly with τ; with independent
seeds they scatter. Common random numbers are a legitimate variance
reduction *for comparisons*, but here the six τ points are the regression's
x-axis, so the fit's residuals are strongly positively correlated and the
reported R² (0.91–0.98) and the phrase "30 fresh grid points" both overstate
the independent information. Each per-model UI fit has **4 points, 2
parameters, 2 residual dof** — and those 4 points are not independent.

## 3. `d = K + #boundary-strata`

### 3.1 The adjudication numbers are real but **have no artifact**. **High.**

`FINDINGS.md` §3 and commit `ce440af` claim
`d = 6.50 / 5.56 / 6.76 vs predicted 6/6/7`. **There is no

`audit/sim_fit_adjudication.py` reconstructs the fit under the frozen recipe

### 3.2 The adjudication is not discriminating. **OVERCLAIMED (High).**

Profiling `d` at fixed integers with `c` refit, and applying **the commit
message's own criterion** ("residuals ≤ 0.75 nats"):

| model | boundary | rule d | fitted d | integers passing ≤0.75 | bootstrap 95 % CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| nano code | 2 | 6 | 5.56 | **5 only** — the predicted 6 gives 0.81 > 0.75 | [3.19, 10.57] |
| mini code | 3 | 7 | 6.76 | **6, 7** | [3.09, 9.55] |

* Under the project's own declared residual criterion the score is
  **2-for-3, not 3-for-3**: on `gpt-4.1-nano` code the *predicted* d = 6
  fails and d = 5 passes.
* The rejected window `[3, 5]` sits **inside the 95 % bootstrap CI** for two

3. `d` drifts systematically **with τ inside a single pool** (nano JSON:
   6.23 → 5.04 as τ moves; 4o-mini JSON: 4.06 → 4.38). A dimension that is a
   property of the *statistic* cannot depend on the threshold. Whatever the
   fits are measuring, it is not a clean `(d/2)·log n` coefficient.
4. Stopping-time `d` and path `d` for the **same pool** disagree: 4o-mini
   JSON gives 3.37 (grid fit) vs 4.06–4.38 (path) vs 5 (rule).

## 4. Conservation / frontier

### 4.1 The window is missed at 2 of 3 margins and the artifact never says so. **High.**

`scripts/run_frontier.py` pre-registers: *"epoch-split lands ON the mixture
frontier (overhead within [0.7, 1.5]x of the UI mixture at matched
margins)"*. Scoring `results_frontier.txt` (the artifact never does this
arithmetic itself):

| τ | UI overhead | epoch-split overhead | ratio | in [0.7, 1.5]? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | +15.47 | +25.90 | **1.674** | **NO** |
| 0.16 | +16.00 | +27.46 | **1.716** | **NO** |
| 0.17 | +18.42 | +27.33 | 1.484 | yes |

**1 of 3.** THEORY.md heads the section *"conservation hypothesis:
SUPPORTED"*, reports the range honestly as "1.48–1.72×" with the window in
parentheses, and then re-anchors the verdict on the one-sided falsification
line ("0.5× — not approached"). That is a post-hoc substitution of a weaker
criterion. Elsewhere in the same document the project logs misses explicitly
(the UI d-window "WINDOW MISSED"; three warm-start misses; "REVERSED (the
informative miss)"), so the omission here is inconsistent with the project's
own standard.

`results_frontier.txt` prints the window in its Reading note but computes no
ratio and prints no PASS/MISS line — unlike `results_overhead_fit.txt`,
which scores its windows.

### 4.2 "within ~1 nat" is false as stated. **Medium.**

THEORY.md: *"The UI mixture's measured overhead matched (d/2)·log n with
d = K + #boundary within ~1 nat at all three margins."* With d = 5:

| τ | median | measured overhead | (5/2)·log n | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 960 | 15.47 | 17.17 | **−1.70** |
| 0.16 | 1498 | 16.00 | 18.28 | **−2.28** |
| 0.17 | 2872 | 18.42 | 19.91 | **−1.49** |

Max gap **2.28 nats**. It is within ±0.46 nats only after fitting a constant
`c = −1.82` — at which point it is a one-parameter fit, not a match.

### 4.3 The one-shot arm's pre-registration half-failed. **Medium.**

Pre-registered: *"fast medians, elevated abstention"*. Observed: abstention
yes (47/200, 0/200, 0/200); medians **not fast** — the only measurable one
(5264 at τ = 0.15) is **5.5× slower** than the UI mixture (960), and is
itself a median over the fastest 47 of 200 paths. THEORY.md reports this as
*"collapsed into the predicted variance-risk corner"*, which keeps the half
that worked.

### 4.4 The one-shot arm is a strawman. **BUG (methodological), High.**

```python
class EpochSplitUICS(StratifiedUICS):
    self._frozen = np.full(k, 0.5)        # initial bet rate
...
("one-shot split", lambda: EpochSplitUICS(k=4, alpha=ALPHA,
                                          first_epoch=40, one_shot=True)),
```

The arm **bets at p = 0.5 for the first 40 samples of every stratum and
charges that to the test martingale**. For a stratum whose true rate is
≈ 0 that is `40·log 2 = 27.7` nats of self-inflicted loss; across K = 4
strata, ≈ **111 nats**, against a crossing level of `log(1/α) = 3.0`. It
cannot win, and its 0–24 % certification is an accounting artifact, not a
property of sample splitting.

Wasserman–Ramdas–Balakrishnan's split-LRT — the ancestor the docstring
cites — **discards** the estimation half; it does not bill it to the
martingale. `audit/sim_frontier_splitlrt.py` implements that faithfully
(burn-in `b` per stratum used only to form a Jeffreys-smoothed `p̂`,
contributing nothing to `log E`, then a frozen plug-in e-process).

Result (100 reps, same pools, same seed offset, same `n ≥ 20, n % 4 == 0`
gating; burn-in samples **are** counted in `n`):

| τ | burn b | certified | median | overhead | ratio to UI mixture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 10 | 20/100 | 500 | +6.62 | 0.428 |
| 0.15 | 25 | 84/100 | 478 | +6.20 | 0.401 |
| 0.15 | 50 | **96/100** | 464 | +5.93 | **0.383** |
| 0.15 | 100 | **100/100** | 574 | +8.05 | **0.520** |
| 0.16 | 25 | 46/100 | 502 | +3.37 | 0.211 |
| 0.16 | 50 | 84/100 | 592 | +4.51 | 0.282 |
| 0.16 | 100 | **100/100** | 712 | +6.03 | **0.377** |
| 0.17 | 50 | 59/100 | 1252 | +6.34 | 0.344 |
| 0.17 | 100 | 89/100 | 1012 | +4.55 | 0.247 |

**The conservation hypothesis's own falsification line is crossed.** At
τ = 0.16 with **100/100 certification** — matching the UI mixture's
200/200, so no censoring advantage whatever — the faithful split-LRT's
overhead is **0.377×** the mixture's, against a pre-registered
"below 0.5× falsifies it". At τ = 0.15 the fully-certifying variant is
0.520× (just above) and the 96 %-certifying variant is 0.383×.

Scope of the falsification, stated fairly: the split's advantage is a
**finite-n** effect. For fixed burn `b` its overhead is
`V·K·b + Θ(n/b)`, minimised at `b* ≈ √(n/(2VK))` for a total
`≈ √(2VKn)` — which grows faster than `(d/2)·log n` and overtakes the
mixture near `n ≈ 5×10³` at these rates. So the *asymptotic* conservation
statement is untouched; what is falsified is the **operationalised test**,
at exactly the `log n ≈ 6–8` regime the paper's headline is about. Since
the entire invention-round argument is a finite-n argument, this matters.

**Validity of the arm (checked, not assumed).** The frozen `p̂_k` is a
function of that stratum's burn-in samples only, so it is predictable:
each per-stratum factor is a nonnegative martingale under its true rate
and the product-with-inf construction is Ville-valid exactly as for the
mixture. Empirical gate at the least-favourable null (per-stratum profile
of the real pool scaled so `p* = τ = 0.16` exactly),
`audit/sim_splitlrt_validity.py`:

| arm | false UNSAFE | rate |
|---|---|---|
| UI mixture (reference) | 0 / 2000, `n_max` 4000 | 0.0000 |
| split-LRT burn = 100 | 0 / 600, `n_max` 1200 | 0.0000 |

Both far below α = 0.05. The split arm is not buying its speed with
invalidity.

### 4.5 The overhead metric is not comparable across arms. **BUG (protocol), High.**

`overhead := median(n)·V − log(1/α)` is computed over **certified paths
only**. An arm that abstains on the slow 80 % of paths gets a flatteringly
small "median" and hence a small "overhead". The falsification line
("below 0.5× falsifies conservation") is therefore reachable by an arm that
simply fails more often — the criterion is ill-posed as written. Either
report a censored-median-adjusted quantity, restrict the comparison to arms
with matched certification fraction, or use `E[min(n, n_max)]`.
